Research interests

We study the atomic nucleus as a window into physics beyond the Standard Model, combining precision laser spectroscopy, ion trapping and quantum sensing of short-lived isotopes and radioactive molecules. At the Texas A&M Cyclotron Institute we are building the Universal Action Spectrometer, a trap-based platform for background-free laser spectroscopy with single-ion sensitivity, to measure nuclear charge radii and electroweak, parity-violating nuclear properties such as the anapole moment. The lab leads an inaugural DOE Genesis Mission award on differentiable digital twins and AI-driven autonomous operation of precision experiments, runs collaborative experiments at FRIB, Argonne, Los Alamos and CERN/ISOLDE, and co-leads the NEPTUNE collaboration.
